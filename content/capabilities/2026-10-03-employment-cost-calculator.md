Most workforce budgets in the Kingdom model social insurance as a flat percentage. It is not flat, and the way it changes is easy to get wrong. A budget built on the wrong schedule is wrong by thousands of riyals per head per year, and the error compounds as headcount grows.

## How the schedule actually moves

Under GOSI's new system, in force since 3 July 2024, the pension contribution for Saudi employees starts at 9% each for employer and employee and rises by half a point every 1 July, reaching 11% each from July 2028. On top of the pension rate, the employer pays 2% for occupational hazards and both sides pay 0.75% for unemployment insurance (SANED).

That gives these totals for a Saudi employee on the new system:

- **July 2025 to June 2026:** employer 12.25%, employee 10.25%.
- **July 2026 to June 2027:** employer 12.75%, employee 10.75%.
- **July 2027 to June 2028:** employer 13.25%, employee 11.25%.
- **From July 2028:** employer 13.75%, employee 11.75%.

Three details change the answer. The steps happen each July, not each January, so a budget year that straddles July has two rates. The steps apply only to people who started contributing on or after 3 July 2024; Saudis who contributed before then stay on a 9% pension rate. And non-Saudi employees carry only the 2% employer charge for occupational hazards. The contribution base is basic salary plus cash housing allowance, capped at SAR 45,000 a month.

## What the calculator does

You choose nationality and, for Saudis, whether they are on the new system. You enter the first month to price, basic salary, housing, other allowances and headcount. It returns the employer cost over twelve months, the first month's employer contribution and employee deduction, the employee's net pay and the loading on gross pay. If a July step falls inside the twelve months, it says so and prices it.

## What it leaves out

End-of-service accrual, medical cover, visa and residency costs, any expatriate levy and training-fund offsets are not included, and housing provided in kind is counted differently by GOSI from a cash allowance. The schedule can also be amended by the Council of Ministers, so check the current position before you budget beyond the dates shown.
